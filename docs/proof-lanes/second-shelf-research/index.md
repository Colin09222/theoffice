## 3. Shipping: USPS Media Mail

**Rates.** Media Mail is priced by weight only, with no zones, and commercial and retail prices are the same:

- **1 lb: $4.39; 2 lb: $5.13; 3 lb: $5.86** (prices effective July 12, 2026). USPS's October 4, 2026 holiday price increase does **not** apply to Media Mail. Source: https://postalguidance.com/media-mail-cost/
- For comparison, Stamps.com still lists the older 2025 prices of $4.63 for 1 lb and $5.38 for 2 lb: https://www.stamps.com/usps/media-mail/
- The official list is USPS Notice 123: https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/Notice123.htm. *I fetched this page, but it was cut off before the Media Mail section, so the $4.39 / $5.13 figures come from the secondary source above.*

**Rules that matter** (USPS Domestic Mail Manual 173, https://pe.usps.com/text/dmm300/173.htm):
- Any fraction of a pound counts as a full pound. A box that weighs 1 lb 1 oz pays the 2 lb rate.
- Books qualify only if they contain **"no advertising matter other than incidental announcements of books."** Bookmarks, tea, candles and other merch would mean the box no longer qualifies for Media Mail.
- Printed matter that is mailable as USPS Marketing Mail may go in loose, so a printed theme card or insert should be fine. Notes that read like personal correspondence need First-Class postage. Short phrases such as "Happy Birthday" are allowed.
- Packages "are not sealed against postal inspection". Sending something at Media Mail rates means agreeing that USPS may inspect it. Postalguidance says ineligible contents can lead to postage due charged to the recipient: https://postalguidance.com/media-mail-cost/
- There is **no delivery-time guarantee**. Postalguidance quotes 2–8 days. Tracking is included, but insurance is not (it starts at $2.80).
- US addresses only. There is no international Media Mail: https://shippinglabel.co/blog/media-mail

**Estimate:** I assume a mass-market paperback weighs about 0.3–0.5 lb and a trade paperback about 0.6–1 lb. If so, 3 books plus packaging would land at **1–3 lb**, or **$4.39–$5.86** postage. Weigh real samples before setting prices.

## 4. Target customer: the budget heavy reader

- **How much Americans read:** The average US adult read **12.6 books** in 2021, down from 15.6 in 2016. **27%** read more than 10 books, and 17% read none. Women averaged **15.7** books and men 9.5. College graduates averaged 14.6. Source: Gallup, Jan 2022, https://news.gallup.com/poll/388541/americans-reading-fewer-books-past.aspx
- **The heaviest readers are reading less.** Gallup found the steepest declines among college graduates, women and adults 55+, the groups that used to read the most. The share of women reading more than 10 books fell from 41% to 32%. (Same source.)
- **Income and reading:** 31% of adults in households earning under $30,000 read no books in the past year, against 15% of those earning $75,000+. Source: Pew Research Center, 2021, https://www.pewresearch.org/short-reads/2021/09/21/who-doesnt-read-books-in-america/
- **Estimate** of market size: 27% of US adults read more than 10 books a year (Gallup). Applied to roughly 260 million US adults (my assumed figure, not taken from a source here), that is on the order of **70 million** heavy readers. Only a small fraction of them would pay for a subscription. Treat this as a ceiling, not a forecast.
- **What they spend:** **I could not confirm** a sourced figure for how much heavy or budget readers spend on books each year.
- **What they complain about:** I found only one real data point. A Cratejoy reviewer of a different book box (Reading Bug Box) wrote "Pricy handling fee AND shipping fee." Source: https://www.cratejoy.com/blogs/box-insider/cheap-book-subscription-boxes. **I could not gather a reliable set of buyer complaints** about used-book boxes, such as repeats, condition or books already read, within this budget. That needs a follow-up look at review sites and Reddit.

## 5. What this means

**Risks**
- **A low-priced rival already exists.** Used Books Monthly sells 4 used books for $18.99 and 1 for $5.99. Second Shelf needs a reason to cost more (the theme and the curation), or it has to match those prices.
- **Thin margins (Estimate).** Books at about $4.50–$12 plus Media Mail at about $4.39–$5.86 gives roughly **$9–$18 per 3-book box** before packaging, labor and payment fees. Against a price of around $15–$19, that leaves little room.
- **Themes make sourcing harder.** Cheap bulk lots are random or sorted by genre. Filling a theme means hand-picking books, which costs time or money.
- **Media Mail limits the box.** Adding merch (bookmarks, tea, swag) means the box no longer qualifies for Media Mail, which would roughly double postage. Delivery is slow and has no guaranteed date.
- **Fewer heavy readers.** Gallup shows the share of heavy readers falling.
- **Repeats and books already read.** These are an obvious risk for any random used-book box. I could not confirm how often buyers complain about them.

**Openings**
- **No theme-led adult used-book box** turned up in what I checked. Competitors pick by genre or send a "blind date" book.
- **Media Mail fits a books-only box well:** $4.39–$5.13 to anywhere in the US, at the same price for a small seller as for a big one. A printed theme card can go in loose.
- **The price gap against new books is large.** Book of the Month gift pricing works out to about $16.67–$20 per new hardcover, while a used book costs about $1.50–$4 at bulk prices (Estimate based on the figures above).
- **Women and college graduates** remain the heaviest reader groups (Gallup), which gives a starting audience to test.

**Next steps for the boss (none of these can be done from here):** weigh 3 sample paperbacks; visit a local Friends of the Library sale and a thrift store to record real per-book prices; read buyer reviews of Used Books Monthly; check the Media Mail price on USPS Notice 123 directly.
