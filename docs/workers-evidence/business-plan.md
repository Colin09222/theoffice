# Good Boy Walks: business plan

*Heat-smart midday walks for busy people in central Austin apartments. This is a short plan built on Arlo's research ([Austin dog walking market research](../good-boy-walks-research/)). Good Boy Walks is new: it has no clients, reviews or track record yet. Every price here is **proposed** until the boss sets it. Every number we worked out is marked **Estimate** and gives its assumption.*

---

## Target customer

- **Who:** professionals in central Austin apartments (Downtown, East Austin, Mueller, South Congress) who work long days or shifts and can't get home at midday. Most likely they have one dog.
- **What they want** (signals from competitors' pages, not survey data): proof that the walk happened (photos and GPS), **the same walker every time**, a walker they can **text directly**, and solo walks rather than group walks.
- **The Austin twist:** weeks of 100°F+ summer heat and hot pavement ([SnoutWalker](https://snoutwalker.com/find/texas/austin)). Owners need walks moved to cooler hours and routes that are safe for paws.
- **Apartment-specific needs** (our reasoning, not yet checked with customers): help getting through building fobs and access, and bonding that covers keys.

## The offer and pricing

**The offer:** a solo 30-minute walk from the same walker every time. Each walk comes with a photo update and a text. **Heat-smart scheduling** is part of the service: from late spring to early autumn, the walk moves to early morning or evening and follows shaded, paw-safe routes, **at no extra charge**. Rest of the year, it's a classic midday walk.

**Why this angle:** none of the four local competitors Arlo checked mentions heat-smart timing or paw-safe routes. Two of them charge extra for exactly the hours a heat-smart walk needs:
- Let's Walk Austin adds $5 before 9 AM or after 5 PM.
- Walk! ATX adds $5 after hours and lists 8 AM–6 PM as its hours.

| Service | Price (**proposed**) | Anchor |
|---|---|---|
| Solo 30-min walk, any time 6 AM–8 PM, weekdays | **$27 (proposed)** | Local independents charge $25–$30. Apps charge about $20–$24 before fees. |
| Weekday plan, 5 walks/week, same walker | **$125/week, i.e. $25/walk (proposed)** | Rewards regular clients and keeps a walker's diary full. |
| Extra dog, same household | **+$8 (proposed)** | Competitors charge +$6 to +$15. |
| Weekend walk | **+$7 (proposed)** | Competitors charge +$5 to +$10. |
| 60-min walk | **$48 (proposed)** | Competitors charge $40 to $55. |

A client who wants a 7 AM walk would pay $27 with us. Let's Walk Austin charges $35 for that walk ($30 + $5), and Walk! ATX $31 ($26 + $5 after hours).

**Biggest risk:** this market is crowded. Hound for Hound has worked the same neighbourhoods since 2010, and the apps are cheaper. We have no reviews. Our edge has to be clear and specific: heat-smart hours at no surcharge, the same walker every time, and direct texting.

**What this means:** one walker at full capacity can plausibly gross about $4,000 a month (**Estimate**), before tax, travel and gear. The hard part is not the cost of running the business. It is winning about 8 regular clients in a crowded market.

- [ ] Draft a short intro for neighbourhood groups and a one-page flyer for apartment managers. *(Team)*
- [ ] Draft a new-client intake form covering the dog's needs, access and fob details, and emergency vet. Don't collect card details on it. *(Team)*

- [ ] Choose how walk updates are sent (photo and text from the walker's phone to start). *(Boss)*

## Only you can do these

Nobody in this office can contact people, sign up for services, post or spend money. These steps are yours:

1. **Register the business.** Choose sole proprietor or LLC, and file with the Texas Secretary of State if you form an LLC. Check austintexas.gov and the Texas.gov Business Portal for any city licence.
2. **Get insurance and bonding.** Talk to an insurance broker about general liability, care, custody and control cover, and a bond that covers keys and fobs.
3. **Take a pet first aid course.** Pick one and book it.
4. **Set up payments.** Open Stripe, create the Payment Link and paste it on the project. Card details are never collected anywhere else.
5. **Post in neighbourhood groups.** Use local Facebook groups, Nextdoor and building community boards for Downtown, East Austin, Mueller and SoCo.
6. **Talk to apartment managers.** Ask about walker access, fob policies and whether they'll share a flyer with residents.
7. **Talk to real dog owners.** Check that heat-smart hours and the same-walker promise matter to them, and that $27 feels right. We have no customer data yet.
8. **Handle anything legal or tax.** That includes sales tax questions, the annual Public Information Report if you form an LLC, and a check of the client terms.
